## Research contribution

For existing dynamic VUS and weighted range precision/recall, derive the local
statistics sufficient to maintain the exact evaluation query as thresholds
change, and separate those identities from compilation and conventional reuse.
The weighted operator tracks fixed-label overlap, fragmentation and moving
positional normalization. Classical component trees, sorting, prefix sums,
closed-form interval overlap and offline bucketing are credited antecedents.
The new claim is the explicit coupled metric state and its consequences, not
the invention of those techniques.

The finite-feature theorem states cardinality-evaluation cost explicitly.
The canonical complete query-associated curve takes O(n log n) arithmetic
operations and matches a comparison-model lower bound. The standard sorting
reduction is not a new lower-bound technique. It does not cover scalar-only
queries, unrestricted algebraic computation or fixed-width radix algorithms.
The implementation covers four canonical positional biases and three
cardinality factors; the more general finite-feature family is theorem-only.

## What changed after the earlier weak accepts

| Objection | Intervention and observed result |
|---|---|
| A flat metric specialization may be too narrow | Implement and prove weighted overlap, cardinality and moving normalization; compare all 12 canonical semantic combinations. Off-label bridges and changing middle-weight normalization are explicit witnesses. |
| Reused data and authored detectors weaken generality | Freeze 26 separately selected WSD/Stock/MSL recordings before outcomes, screen against prior provenance, and generate 52 profiles through unchanged public FFT/COPOD APIs. MSL has only two recordings; hidden physical-source dependence remains possible. |
| A fast kernel may not improve a real consumer | Substitute only the range-curve callback in native TimeEval, preserving loading, three requested metrics, default/query handling and CSV writing. Exhaustive combined workflows improve on both hosts; default combined gains are small and not consistently supported across executions. |
| Raster may be a weak conventional algorithm | Add a separately frozen closed-form interval baseline and remeasure raster. The exhaustive incremental gain survives both controls. Interval is often slower than raster, so the faster combined raster comparison leads the headline. |
| One host or a favorable tied winner may explain the result | Regenerate inputs and public scores on Linux; reconstruct saved curves and all native outputs independently. All 312 case/semantic grid winner sets are singletons, so worst-tie sensitivity equals the original best-tie regret on the Mac panel. |

## Principal strengthened result

The unchanged TimeEval read-to-result-write consumer requests ROC-AUC, PR-AUC
and range PR-AUC for all 52 public profiles. Six method permutations measure
each method/query/batch combination. Ratios below are matched geometric ratios;
brackets are approximate 95% paired-order timing intervals conditional on this
fixed workload and host.

| Query contract and comparator | Apple M1 Pro | Fresh Linux x86_64 |
|---|---:|---:|
| All native observed queries: raster / incremental | **2.732 [2.656, 2.812]** | **2.756 [2.742, 2.773]** |
| All native observed queries: interval / incremental | 3.034 [2.990, 3.075] | 3.190 [3.166, 3.224] |
| Native nominal-50 default: raster / incremental | 1.026 [.998, 1.049] | 1.016 [.997, 1.033] |

The separate original public-reference Linux replay also completed successfully.
Its combined exhaustive raster/incremental ratio is **1.932 [1.907, 1.946]**,
smaller than the supplemental control replay. The original Mac value is
2.728 [2.649, 2.801]. Thus the observed range over all four study executions is
**1.93–2.76 times**, without pooling. Identical original/supplemental Linux score
hashes do not make the timing runs identical. The within-run intervals do not
cover this between-run variation or identify its causal source. Original and
supplemental Linux OpenBLAS dispatch labels differ (Cooperlake/SkylakeX), despite
matching core package versions and shared runtime sources. The original Linux
default ratio is 1.014 [1.007,1.027]; the other three default combined intervals
include one. The original Linux MSL exhaustive interval also includes one,
retaining its slowest bucket trial.

At all observed queries, median whole-workflow times are 2.947/3.275/1.073
seconds for Mac raster/interval/incremental and 4.113/4.755/1.499 seconds on
Linux. Ratios are not computed by dividing these displayed medians. The
supplemental Linux machine's CPU model was not captured; it must not be
identified with the AMD EPYC machine used in the earlier study.

The separately frozen weighted operator comparison supports all three public
collections against the interval control:

| Collection | Apple exhaustive interval / sweep | Linux exhaustive interval / sweep |
|---|---:|---:|
| WSD, 12 recordings | 7.323 [7.277, 7.342] | 12.945 [12.396, 12.991] |
| Stock, 12 recordings | 26.339 [26.244, 26.387] | 36.149 [35.977, 36.224] |
| MSL, 2 recordings | 1.706 [1.679, 1.728] | 2.381 [2.347, 2.418] |

These intervals adjust over three collections per contract and comparator.
They do not establish population generalization. A separate exploratory
post-hoc reconstruction of the remeasured raster contrast gives exhaustive
ratios 8.608/21.991/1.421 on Mac and 11.621/26.717/1.726 on Linux, with all six
host-specific adjusted intervals above one. Its adjustment covers six
collection/query contrasts per host, not the later choice to inspect this
comparator. Every prespecified semantic result remains available.

The original Linux weighted replay independently reconstructs all 11,232
records and 156 curves, with maximum error 2.498e-15 and no same-contract
winner disagreement. Its original raster/sweep exhaustive ratios are
7.460 [7.367,7.484] for WSD, 15.848 [15.628,15.875] for Stock and
1.670 [1.659,1.682] for MSL. MSL id7 loses (.859), and ten of the MSL
grid sweep/bucket semantic intervals include one. All 312 Linux grid winner
sets are also singleton, with maximum regret .001412 and none at .01.

## Boundaries reached

- MSL id7 loses against the faster conventional control on both hosts:
  .563 times on Mac and .684 times on Linux. Its few distinct queries make
  repeated scanning competitive. A narrow aggregate timing interval does not
  remove this recording-level loss.
- On the separate public-detector panel, maximum selection regret is only
  .001412, with no case reaching the prespecified .01 practical cutoff.
  The earlier 15/73 disjoint winner-set result cannot become a prevalence
  claim about the new detectors or unseen data.
- Default combined TimeEval gains over the faster compiled control are small
  and not consistently supported across executions. The earlier nine-metric VUS-only replacement remains
  **1.031 times** end to end. Its unfavorable result is retained.
- Mac and Linux regenerate their own score outputs. All FFT profile hashes
  agree, while 25 COPOD hashes differ. Inputs, query counts and timing
  uncertainty are reported by host; this is not an isolated CPU intervention.
- Censored public calls do not have completed timings or verified outputs.
  Compiled-control parity does not replace a missing public-reference output.
- The contribution remains a specialized exact analytics operator. This work
  does not measure an uninspected GPU evaluator or establish detector-accuracy
  SOTA. More repeats of the same panel cannot resolve conceptual significance.
