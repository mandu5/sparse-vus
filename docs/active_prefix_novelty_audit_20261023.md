**Decision for the focused incremental paper:** the inspected prior algorithms do not disclose the complete active-prefix construction. The manuscript has a defensible, narrow algorithmic distinction. It does **not** presently establish a broadly superior evaluator or a substantial advance merely by adding the singleton input-reading lower bound. All three predeclared primary runtime tests failed; the strongest incremental practical gains are secondary-width results against the author's own previous evaluator. Those facts should determine the incremental claim, not be relegated to qualifications.

**Important scope correction after inspecting publication provenance:** the public v0.3 software and working manuscript are not established peer-reviewed publications. A coherent first research paper can include the support-event reduction, exact inactive-run ROC integration, geometry and fixed-key complexity, then present saturation as an extension. Treating all those original contributions as scientifically unavailable because the same author released code two days earlier would artificially weaken the paper. ## Adversarial assessment of the current contribution

**1. The saturation test is simple algebra, not an independently deep algorithmic discovery.** Substitution of `T=A+B` and `P=P0+B/2` into capped recall immediately yields `A+B/2 >= P0`; the existence multiplier separately requires `H=K_w`. The zero PR suffix and height-one ROC tail are immediate once recall stays one. What needs proof and can carry novelty is achieving the resulting work bound without first constructing every width's full support arrays. The manuscript's acknowledgement of ordinary cutoff prior art is appropriate.

**2. The data structures are established; the composition is the candidate contribution.** Reversible deletion/restoration is explicitly described in [Knuth's Dancing Links record](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0011047), whose abstract was inspected here. This audit did not reread its full proof or independently audit the cited union-find/radix references. The manuscript correctly treats those ingredients as standard. A reviewer can reasonably regard their combination as competent algorithm engineering unless the new bound or consequences are sufficiently general and useful.

**3. The singleton upper bound is useful but admits a much simpler competing construction.** This is a deduction from the manuscript, not located prior art or measured software:

1. Obtain complete global score ranks once using the same fixed-key preparation.
2. For a singleton original anomaly, its support is a nested interval. As radius increases, at most two new positions enter.
3. Maintain only the three highest active scores, updating this constant-size set per entering position.
4. At each width, evaluate those positions in descending order and stop at saturation; retain full global counts for their events.

With distinct active scores, the mass lemma guarantees saturation within these three positions. This gives `O(n+L)` time with no reversible active list and no union-find. Thus the singleton theorem is not evidence that the general data structure is necessary. A small, independent implementation of this construction would be a strong adversarial comparator and a clearer explanation of the theorem. It must be labeled a newly constructed baseline, not attributed to prior authors.

**4. The lower bound is input-inspection optimality, not a difficult evaluation lower bound.** Changing one unread off-support score changes every width's PR from one to one-half on the proposed boundary witness. That is a clean adversary. It establishes unavoidable score reading under the stated deterministic coordinate-query model; it does not prove that sorting, `ΣJ_w` traversal, union-find, or `L` separate evaluations are required. The manuscript appropriately avoids those stronger conclusions. Calling the result simply “optimal VUS” would be misleading.

**5. The asymptotic advantage has a specific parameter regime.** For one original interval, `Q <= P0+L` (clipped by `n`). The previous radix bound is therefore `O(n+LP0+L²)` and the distinct-score prefix bound is `O(n+LP0)`. For a singleton, the comparison is `O(n+L²)` versus `O(n+L)`. At fixed `L=30`, both are already `O(n)`; a meaningful asymptotic distinction requires growing widths, with `L²` large relative to `n` for the previous width work to dominate. A singleton theorem alone does not explain broad speedups at ordinary small widths.

**6. Two restrictions are substantive.** Multiple components can postpone coverage until a low-scoring component is reached; small `P0` therefore does not imply a small global prefix. Large active ties defeat the position-wise singleton bound. The manuscript supplies both caveats honestly, but the resulting optimal subclass is narrow. Equal scores are particularly relevant to quantized or rounded score outputs; the FF-VUS wrapper provides a concrete prior example of score rounding, although its metric contract also differs.

**7. The measured practical contribution is modest relative to the closest controls.** The checked result file reports all three `L=30` primary criteria failing. At external `L=300`, median ratios against released radix are 1.506× on Mac and 1.627× on Linux. Against the per-case fastest measured control, those medians fall to 1.113× and 1.142×, with 9/48 and 10/48 losses. Against matched last-positive stopping, they are 1.136× and 1.152×. These distinguish a useful secondary-regime improvement from a general performance breakthrough. The much larger library-baseline ratios combine earlier improvements with the new stopping mechanism. No new timings were run for this audit.

None of these objections proves that the work is unpublishable. They do mean that adding a standard input-reading lower bound does not by itself resolve novelty/significance concerns, and an acceptance prediction would have no evidential basis.

## Strongest falsifiable claim currently supported

> For the pinned original exhaustive scalar VUS target, finite binary64 score keys, word-sized indexing, and unit-cost exact metric arithmetic, a radius-activated score-order schedule with incremental component bookkeeping evaluates both integrals in `O(n+L+α(K)ΣJ_w)` time and `O(n+Q)` auxiliary words, where `J_w` includes the complete first mass-and-coverage-saturating active tie. There is no additional width-by-maximum-support preparation term. For one original interval with distinct active scores, this yields `O(n+LP0)` time for every score ordering. For a singleton and `L<=n`, the resulting `O(n)` time matches a deterministic `Ω(n)` score-query lower bound. The inspected prior implementations do not establish this bound. Practical superiority at the predeclared default width was not demonstrated.

This can be falsified by a hidden per-width support/component scan, an exact-arithmetic semantic counterexample, failure of the bound on an allowed tie/geometry case, or an earlier disclosure of an equivalent guarantee. It does not depend on claiming the first fast VUS, first support restriction, first cumsum evaluator, first recall cutoff, new radix sorting, universal superiority, or bitwise floating-point exactness.

## Changes that would materially strengthen the research

1. **Test the obvious simplification.** Add the constant-size top-three singleton evaluator as a separately written, parity-tested comparator in a new experiment. Extend to top `ceil(2sqrt(2)P0)` for one interval if worthwhile. This tests whether the general machinery adds value in the very subclass used for the strongest headline. No such implementation or experiment was performed in this audit.
2. **Broaden the theorem where the actual obstruction lies.** Seek bounds or a different algorithm for multiple components and complete ties. A descriptive `E_w` parameter is mathematically valid but merely names the difficult coverage prefix. More substantial progress would bound or avoid that work under a useful structural assumption, or establish a lower bound showing when it is unavoidable. Investigate aggregation within tied events rather than presenting the current tied-input failure as a problem lower bound.
3. **Make numerical exactness a separate deliverable.** The implementation can round a stopping inequality differently from exact arithmetic. A certified predicate or a proved error/fallback policy near saturation would materially strengthen the connection between an exact theorem and finite-precision software. Existing small discrepancies are good evidence, but are not an all-input stopping certificate.
4. **Target a fresh, justified workload.** Preserve the failed primary decision. If large widths or repeated same-label evaluation are the intended application, predeclare that new workload, explain why users need it, and measure complete costs against the fastest credible same-task CPU alternatives. Reuse geometry across many score vectors if that application motivates it, while charging score-dependent work. Do not relabel the existing secondary result as a successful primary result.
5. **Explain gains with a predictive cost model.** Use preparation cost, active ties, `P0`, `Q_w`, coverage delay, and measured traversal counts to predict a crossover or select a fixed dispatcher using development data. Validate on new cases; the already computed fastest-control oracle is not a deployable selector. A reliable small-width fallback plus clear large-width benefit can be more useful than another large ratio against slow upstream loops.
6. **Keep the semantic comparison reproducible.** Include the actual source pins and minimal witnesses for threshold sampling, tie handling, ramp shape, overlap and component adjacency. If adapting FF-VUS to the original target, explicitly label that adaptation and validate its equality contract before timing. An unknown GPU comparison should remain unknown.

The first two items address scientific depth; the next four address trustworthiness and practical significance. Merely adding more favorable synthetic cases or more runs against the official nested-loop implementation would not resolve the main objections.

This distinction matters for the contemplated venue. Current [PVLDB publication policy §3.1.1](https://vldb.org/pvldb/general-information/publication-policies) defines prior publication in terms of accepted peer-reviewed proceedings or refereed journals, and explicitly permits website postings and technical reports. That policy supports presenting a unified first paper based on the public software precursor; it does not certify novelty, compliance with every possible venue rule, or acceptance.

**The earlier assessment must therefore be read as incremental, not as a verdict on the whole Sparse VUS research program.** It is appropriate to say “the present active-prefix extension does not newly invent support-event compression.” It is inappropriate to infer “support-event compression cannot contribute to this author's first peer-reviewed paper.” Public chronology and research contribution are different questions.

A stronger coherent narrative is:

1. Derive the exact scalar event representation for the original dynamic VUS semantics: skip score groups without support while preserving global ties, PR contributions and the ROC rectangle before the next active transition.
2. Compose that representation with the two-nearest-interval geometry and existing ranking methods, proving the comparison-model and fixed-key preparation bounds.
3. Add width activation, component handling and prefix stopping as an adaptive refinement, including the single-interval guarantees and honest failure regimes.
4. Separate **whole-evaluator comparisons** against external implementations and matched dense algorithms from **incremental comparisons** against the author's preceding support-event implementation and stopping controls.

The first two steps already avoid work that remains in the inspected official, Klein and Benchopt algorithms. The exact ROC identity is more specific than generic support restriction or simply deleting ROC points: dynamic denominators and global ties must survive event removal. The two-distance lemma is short, and radix sorting is standard; neither should be inflated into an independent foundational breakthrough. The substantive candidate is their exact, proved evaluator composition and measured consequences. No inspected external source supplies the same complete construction or guarantee; this remains a bounded originality finding, not firstness certification.

The evidence also changes the performance interpretation. The v0.3 working manuscript reports a separate frozen experiment on 308 series/17 families, with primary-width PR median ratios 20.92×/14.54× against Klein and 2.60×/4.79× against matched nearest-distance dense radix on Mac/Linux. Its source describes both five-control primary criteria passing. Those are prior experiment reports, not newly reproduced results here. The current external paired supplement reports 34.918× median versus Benchopt on all 48 cases; DTAI's completed 10-case subset has 101.106× median with two prescribed cases censored. The latter cannot be described as a 12-case median. These support a bounded whole-evaluator efficiency claim, while the three failed default-width tests directly constrain the additional value of saturation. They are not contradictory results and must not be pooled or swapped between claims.

**Supported whole-paper claim:**

> Sparse VUS derives an exact support-event representation for a pinned exhaustive scalar VUS target and combines it with interval geometry and standard score-ranking methods. The construction gives `O(n log(Q+1)+(L+1)Q)` comparison-based time and `O(n+(L+1)Q)` fixed-binary64 time; a further active-prefix organization yields `O(n+L+α(K)ΣJ_w)`. Incremental saturation fails its default-width primary performance criterion and gives qualified gains at larger widths. This is a materially stronger research proposition than a paper whose entire contribution is “stop when recall reaches one.” It is plausibly original relative to the inspected external algorithms. Whether its combined algorithmic and empirical contribution is substantial enough for a particular venue remains a review judgment. “Fastest exact VUS,” “global SOTA,” or “first optimal VUS” still exceeds the evidence, especially given the unmeasured exact-target GPU comparison.

Recommended precursor wording: **“A preliminary implementation and working manuscript of the support-event evaluator were publicly released as Sparse VUS v0.3.0 on 21 October 2026. ## Proposed tied-score extension: novelty boundary and numerical contract

During this review, the parent agent reported an implementation in progress for one original interval: represent complete score classes by their temporal runs, activate a class at its first active radius, and evaluate each visited class's ramp mass from differences of root-prefix sums. The proposed work measure is `O(n+L+Σ_visited R_g)`, where `R_g` is the run cost of a visited score class at a width, with `O(n+L)` auxiliary words. This paragraph records a proposal, **not a verified theorem or measured result**; the new implementation and proof were not inspected in this audit.

That direction addresses a genuine limitation of the current position-wise theorem. A bound on the number of visited **score classes**, together with bounded run complexity, can permit large ties without enumerating every tied position. For a singleton, a constant number of visited classes with constant runs each would give linear total work even though the ties may be large. It would not establish linear time for every arbitrary tied arrangement unless total visited run cost is also bounded. A spatially alternating score class can have many runs. Clearly define whether `R_g` counts all class runs or only active intersecting runs and charge any search needed to find them.

The numerical idea reportedly uses three `uint64` limbs to store exact prefix sums of scaled, rounded square-root values, then subtracts prefixes exactly. **Wide fixed-point accumulators and exact summation are established prior techniques:**

- [Radford M. Neal, *Fast Exact Summation Using Small and Large Superaccumulators* (2015), abstract and introduction](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1505.05571) explicitly describes fixed-point superaccumulators made of 64-bit chunks and exact sums subsequently rounded to floating point. These sections were read directly.
- [Iakymchuk, Collange, Defour and Graillat, *ExBLAS: Reproducible and Accurate BLAS Library*, NRE2015 author presentation](https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/itl/ssd/is/NRE-2015-04-iakymchuk.pdf), the multi-level summation and scalar-superaccumulator slides, explicitly combine floating-point expansions/error-free transformations with Kulisch accumulators. This is a presentation, not misidentified here as a journal paper.

The possible contribution is the metric-specific temporal-run decomposition and resulting work guarantee, plus a correct numerical realization; it is not invention of a fixed-point or multiword accumulator. Prefix differences are standard range-sum queries. A newly needed bounded-width accumulator proof is still necessary for the supported input/width domain.

Exact addition/subtraction of rounded roots eliminates cancellation **in that stored sum**, but does not make irrational roots exact, establish correctly rounded final VUS, or ensure identity with the official floating-point evaluation of `sqrt(1-d/w)`. Rewriting that expression as `sqrt(w-d)/sqrt(w)` is exact over reals and can differ after rounding. The paper should separately state (i) the real-arithmetic identity and operation-count theorem, (ii) the exact fixed-point sum contract, (iii) root/scale/division error bounds or measured numerical tolerance, and (iv) the stopping-predicate policy near equality. Prior ExBLAS correctness does not automatically transfer to this composed evaluator.
