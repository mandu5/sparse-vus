# Independent scientific review D — immutable manuscript v2

**Overall recommendation: Accept. Internal quality score: 7/10. Confidence: high on the inspected correctness and saved-evidence claims, moderate on broader novelty and practical generality.** This is a simulated scientific review, not an external PVLDB decision or an acceptance probability.

The paper is a publishable, scoped contribution to exact evaluation of stored anomaly-score profiles. Its strongest result is the metric-specific state that maintains weighted range trajectories, accompanied by meaningful gains over compiled conventional controls when many thresholds are requested. The support-event VUS analysis is useful, but its large public-code speedups should not be mistaken for the isolated contribution of its geometry. The final manuscript makes that distinction adequately.

**Largest weakness:** conceptual novelty and practical benefit are both bounded. Much of the machinery is established component-tree, prefix-sum, sorting, and work-sharing technique; substantial native-consumer improvement appears chiefly under exhaustive queries. Default-query benefits are small, and the separate public-detector panel provides little evidence of materially better detector selection. These limits keep my score at 7 rather than 8.

The internal scale is explicitly separate from any official PVLDB form: 1–3 unsound, 4–5 below the publication bar, 6 requiring material scientific revision, 7 a publishable scoped contribution with limitations, 8 strong, and 9–10 exceptional. **Synopsis.** The paper derives two different sufficient-state representations. Dynamic VUS removes inactive support events while preserving ROC's omitted predecessor rectangles, complete global ties, and width-dependent geometry. Weighted Tatbul range metrics maintain true-interval overlap/fragmentation and predicted-component precision during threshold activations and merges; fixed-label feature prefixes support moving positional normalization. The paper separates these identities from conventional implementation improvements and evaluates both operator-level and native-consumer behavior.

**Strengths.**

1. The exact-output contracts and their differences are explicit: dynamic VUS integration, strict-query weighted trajectories, and native inclusive TimeEval queries with synthetic endpoints are not conflated.
2. The weighted local-state analysis addresses the difficult part of the metric: overlap, fragmentation, and moving component normalization change together. The theorem states the cost of evaluating cardinality factors and separates exact arithmetic from binary64 behavior.
3. The empirical comparisons include same-compiler raster and closed-form interval recomputation. The faster control, low-query losses, public timeouts, unsuccessful alternatives, and between-run variability remain visible.
4. Native TimeEval evaluation includes score reads, all three requested metrics, and result writing. Saved curves and actual CSVs permit substantive independent verification; the final process-session experiment is finite, separately frozen, and reported without replacing previous results.

**Weaknesses.**

1. The novelty is specialized. Component-tree construction, incremental component attributes including fixed-target conditioning, positional overlap formulas, and offline threshold processing are established antecedents.
2. Practical importance is concentrated in exhaustive evaluation. The nine-metric consumer improves only about 1.031×; native default-query gains are about 1–3%; the separate panel's maximum observed F1 selection regret is only about 0.00141.
3. Generality remains limited: two public detector implementations, 26 separately selected recordings, only two MSL recordings, and narrow native callback semantics. Repeating processes on one runner adds no independent domains or hosts.
4. Evidence coverage is unequal. Public native-reference comparisons complete for 28/52 profiles per contract, not Stock or the combined public workflow. Other complete outputs are supported by compiled-control agreement and separate operator checks. Conditional timing intervals cannot resolve workload generalization, and exploratory comparator intervals do not undo post-hoc selection.

## Detailed analysis

1. **The VUS contraction argument preserves the right information.** An inactive threshold changes the global predicted count while leaving the weighted true-positive quantities and existence state unchanged. Its PR contribution vanishes, but its ROC contribution must be retained as a rectangle using the preceding recall. The four-point example correctly exposes the error in joining only retained endpoints. Complete tie groups require the global counts on both sides of the tie. The two-distance argument is also sound for the pinned clipped square-root target: two active ramps already saturate an off-label location, so more distant intervals add no information. I found no missing event case in this reasoning. This is not a proof for every metric called VUS or every interpolation convention.

2. **The weighted recurrence is correct within its stated assumptions.** A point activation inside a true interval changes that interval's fragment count by one minus the number of active immediate neighbors inside it. Predicted components are updated by removing at most two adjacent components and adding the merged component. Their overlap and number of intersected true intervals can be reconstructed from fixed-label prefixes and endpoint information. The middle positional bias needs the changing midpoint split; a single undifferentiated moment would not suffice, and the manuscript handles that distinction. Finite-feature evaluation costs and the nonconstant exact-arithmetic cost of the improved cardinality factor are accounted for. The comparison lower bound concerns a complete trajectory associated with arbitrary input queries; it does not establish the same lower bound for a single maximum-F1 scalar, anonymous outputs, or fixed-word radix sorting. Those qualifications are present.

3. **The closest conventional antecedents substantially narrow novelty, but do not duplicate the full claimed state.** [Tatbul et al.](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2018/file/8f468c873a32bb0619eaeb2050ba45d1-Paper.pdf) already give positional weights, cardinality penalties, and optimized ordered/closed-form overlap evaluation. The classical [Najman–Couprie component-tree work](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIP.2006.877518) and [Menotti et al.'s one-dimensional construction](https://hal.science/hal-00622373) establish that threshold components and attributes are old machinery. [Naegel and Passat](https://www.ipol.im/pub/art/2014/71/) incrementally compute component attributes relative to a fixed segmentation target; generic label conditioning is therefore not new. [Ranefall and Wählby](https://doi.org/10.1002/cyto.a.22806) derive threshold-dependent object precision/recall from component-size attributes, with a size-defined target and a different recall denominator. The final paper acknowledges all these points. Its remaining distinction is the joint Tatbul overlap/fragmentation state with moving positional normalization, plus the separate VUS event identity. I judge that combination useful and sufficiently specific for a scoped algorithms paper, but not a broad new component-tree framework.

4. **The recent literature inventory is coverage evidence, not fourteen competing algorithms.** I inspected `artifacts/pvldb-research-20261024/strengthening/literature-strengthened-14-v1.json` and primary pages, abstracts, and relevant sections for its fourteen 2024–2026 entries: VUS, PATE, TSB-AD, TAB, TSB-AutoAD, the formal metric analysis, CCE, the taxonomy, adversarial reporting analysis, RAMSAD, Sylligardos's acceleration work, mTSBench, ESA, and the one-liner analysis. Most address metric design, benchmarks, selection, or reporting rather than the same exact computational query. I did not read every one of those papers at proof-level depth. The [2025 VUS paper](https://www.paparrizos.org/papers/BoniolVLDBJ25.pdf), current exact public evaluators, and the older component-tree/Tatbul literature are the closer computational antecedents. The final manuscript does not turn the inventory into an unsupported universal-precedence claim.

5. **The strongest inspected controls are appropriate, and attribution is now proportionate.** Comparing only with slow public Python code would be insufficient. Here the same-compiler raster and interval implementations retain complete outputs and hold the query set fixed. The interval control uses conventional closed-form overlap at each query without cross-query metric state. Raster is frequently faster than interval and is not hidden. For VUS, a direct support-cumsum control is faster than the released nearest-distance evaluator in the inspected collections; shared preparation accounts for much of the subsequent batch increment. The retained negative control prevents attributing every speedup to the geometry lemma. A bare component tree is not itself an equivalent Tatbul output evaluator; augmenting one with the same coupled state is close to the proposed algorithmic formulation. I do not require a nominally different implementation of that same state as an additional mandatory baseline. The paper also correctly avoids a GPU-superiority claim over semantically different or unmeasured implementations; [Sylligardos's acceleration discussion](https://www.vldb.org/2025/Workshops/VLDB-Workshops-2025/PhD/PhD25_17.pdf) makes such a universal claim particularly inappropriate.

6. **There is a real consumer and a bounded reason to care.** [TimeEval's issue 22](https://github.com/TimeEval/TimeEval/issues/22) documents expensive range evaluation and threshold sampling; it supports the use case, not adoption of this implementation or a claim of urgent current demand. Exhaustive native evaluation benefits by roughly 1.93–2.76× over raster in the four original/supplemental executions and 1.919× in the five-session follow-up. On the supplemental native workload, exhaustive incremental evaluation costs close to its sampled version while answering a different, more complete query. That is useful even though the separate panel rarely changes the selected detector. The paper does not establish that ordinary users universally need exhaustive evaluation, or that detector training and deployment become substantially faster. The native integration's flat/cardinality-one range PR-AUC contract is correctly distinguished from the broader weighted operator study.

7. **The final follow-up supports repeatability, with no causal or population upgrade.** From the raw duration records I reconstructed exhaustive raster/bucket 1.919024946 with adjusted descriptive interval [1.905809850, 1.932157088], and interval/bucket 2.216015946 [2.200816732, 2.227253451]. Default50 values are 1.016773168 [1.011235558, 1.024668408] and 1.022602648 [1.017350941, 1.030161644]. All five processes and all 180 scheduled timed calls complete, with all six method permutations per contract/block. These are empirical five-block bootstrap intervals, not a guaranteed-coverage confidence procedure for five independent hosts. V2's first fresh generation happens to match all prior Linux score/query records exactly. The preserved v1 preparation failure involved 25 COPOD differences and one cardinality change; no timed metric result was available to select v2. The manuscript accurately retains that history and does not claim the new run explains the prior 1.932× versus 2.756× difference.

## Calibration, required revisions, and future work

The form above adapts the actual [published historical PVLDB review fields](https://dbis.cs.tu-dortmund.de/publikationen/2020/data-parallel-query-processing-on-non-uniform-data/vldb-2020-reviews/), not the unavailable current private CMT form. Its [revision reviews](https://dbis.cs.tu-dortmund.de/publikationen/2020/data-parallel-query-processing-on-non-uniform-data/vldb-2020-revision-reviews/) make complete-consumer benefit, conventional comparators, and explanation of unfavorable regimes useful calibration questions. They do not imply this paper deserves the same editorial outcome.

I also read the permitted `public-calibration.md` accepted/rejected adjacent-venue cases. Their objections about scope, missing strong baselines, and attribution are relevant here; their numerical ratings are not transferable to PVLDB. The provenance limitation on mirrored OpenReview notes remains material. My recommendation follows this manuscript's proofs and evidence, not a rating average or publication analogy.

**Necessary scientific revisions for acceptance of this exact PDF: none identified.** I do not find an unresolved correctness, comparator, or claim/evidence mismatch requiring a Revise recommendation. The limited importance and generality are already disclosed and are reflected in the score. Artifact availability and normal submission administration should be checked by the submitting author; this review does not attest to an actual submission or venue acceptance.

**Optional presentation improvement:** a shorter consolidated explanation of when a user should request exhaustive evaluation could make the practical case easier to see. This is not a condition of my recommendation, and the current output-contract map already prevents the main semantic ambiguity.

**Future work, not required revision:** wider detector and multivariate coverage; an independently motivated application where exhaustive evaluation changes a consequential choice; and a prospectively specified rule that chooses raster or incremental evaluation from query/support structure, tested on new workloads. More repetitions of the same five-session experiment would not by themselves address the largest weakness.

My final judgment is **Accept, internal 7/10**, for the exact specialized operators and their measured operating range. The final PDF supports that contribution without needing a claim of universal acceleration, general detector-selection improvement, or fundamentally new component-tree machinery.
