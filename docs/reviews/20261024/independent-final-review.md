## Summary and strengths

The paper replaces exhaustive threshold evaluation of one precisely specified dynamic VUS operator with a support-event representation. It preserves complete global tie counts and accounts for omitted horizontal ROC segments. Two nearest-interval distances suffice for the clipped ramps. Fixed binary64 radix preparation then gives `O(n + (L+1)Q)` work, with explicit input-sized memory. A batch implementation shares label geometry across score vectors, with a separately measured, smaller incremental gain.

The strongest aspects are:

1. **A real operator-preserving reduction.** The ROC predecessor term is necessary, and the distinction from zero-area PR omission is demonstrated by a concrete counterexample. The target's dynamic denominator, overlap convention and complete ties are explicit.
2. **Relevant, matched comparisons.** The compiled dense control uses the same radix ordering and two-distance geometry, making the 3.54–3.96× primary dense/stream ratios more informative than a Python-versus-Numba comparison. Klein is measured exhaustively. Benchopt is also compared against a two-output evaluator, avoiding an unfair ROC+PR versus PR-only headline.
3. **Substantial measured whole-method gains with honest attribution.** The width-30 case ratios against Klein are 25.17–30.51×, while the released/stream ratios are 1.207–1.496×. Direct collection latencies, not sums of case medians, support the workflow claims. The manuscript correctly assigns the former to the whole method and the latter to sharing.
4. **Useful approximation evidence without changing the target.** At 250 thresholds, 14/73 numerical winner sets differ and four cases have exact VUS-PR regret at least 0.01; at 1,000 thresholds only three sets differ and none reaches 0.01. The retained zero-loss NAB results prevent a one-sided narrative.
5. **Unusually explicit qualifications.** The paper acknowledges reused recordings, nine SMD label blocks, transductive score generation, small stream/geometry differences, warm timing, and higher batch memory. The dense synthetic case gives stream 225.0 MiB versus released 175.2 MiB, so the space tradeoff is not concealed.

## Correctness and algorithmic novelty

I found no fatal error in the stated principal theorem. At fixed width, an inactive admitted point changes only `C`; therefore the horizontal ROC increments telescope at the previous recall and the PR increment is zero. The complete-tie transition then uses the global before/after counts. The ROC tail to the explicit `(1,1)` endpoint remains necessary and is implemented in [core.py, lines 120–125](../../../src/sparse_vus/core.py). The two-distance lemma follows because any active ramp is at least `1/sqrt(2)`, so two contributions already clip to one. Original positives remain one. The comparison-model bound and the fixed-word bound are distinguished correctly. The latter is not a claim about arbitrary-real comparison sorting, arbitrary-precision arithmetic, or bitwise equality with the reference.

The shared-call time and conservative space bounds agree with [batch.py](../../../src/sparse_vus/batch.py): the two model-by-support arrays are real retained costs, and the public finite-value check/conversion can allocate model-by-time temporaries. The paper properly avoids attributing an asymptotic per-model improvement to loop interchange. The optional saturation and run-aggregation constructions are not needed for the main theorem; their earlier proofs should not be counted as additional verified main contributions of this review.

The **defensible novelty sentence** is: *For the pinned dynamic VUS operator, complete global ranks plus support-event integration and two-distance clipped-ramp geometry remove per-width off-support and exhaustive-threshold-array work while preserving the exact PR and corrected ROC scalars.* Support localization, cumulative sums, radix sorting, full-recall stopping and caching individually predate this manuscript.

I inspected Boniol et al.'s optimized Algorithms 2/3 and current Klein/Benchopt code, not merely their package descriptions. Klein already computes support cumsums, but `.cache/fast_vus/fast_vus.py:67–111` still constructs global threshold arrays and full-series coverage per width. Benchopt's pinned `_range_auc_volume` performs full-series cumsums and threshold-sized integration arrays. This establishes a specific distinction; claiming that previous work had no support reuse would be false. Four public repository HEAD queries on the review date matched the manuscript's pins: official VUS `467bace0`, Klein `1bae859f`, Benchopt `2b55ccb1`, aVUSi `facf4362`.

The semantic exclusion of native FF-VUS from equal-output timing ratios is justified by the [retained witnesses](../../../artifacts/ff_vus_audit_20261021.json) and inspected audit/wrapper code. For example, matched physical radius still gives different ramp weights and a PR discrepancy of about 0.00956; score rounding can change a width-zero result from 1.0 to 0.5. This does **not** establish CPU/GPU superiority over a target-matched adaptation of that approach. The manuscript avoids that claim.

## Numbered weaknesses and falsifiable remediation

1. **The runtime evidence does not yet explain the operating range of the principal algorithm.** This is the main reason for Revise. The new real-data study uses `n = 1,000–28,696`, `M = 8`, `K <= 12`, and width-30 `Q/n <= 0.223`; all collections occupy favorable sparse-support regimes. The `n = 500,000` cases have one centered interval and measure process RSS, explicitly not runtime. The `M = 32` case repeats existing scores and is likewise a resource test. Thus neither an empirical runtime crossover as `Q` approaches `n`, fragmented-label behavior, nor model-count scaling is established. The direct workflow is useful, but its three stream medians are only 0.0470, 0.0528 and 0.2824 seconds. Large ratios alone do not show when this changes a substantial data-management workload. Sources: manuscript body lines 329–360, 470–494; [resource protocol](../../../artifacts/pvldb-research-20261024/resources/protocol.json); Tables 3 and 6.

   **Remediation:** freeze a separate, bounded timing study varying `n`, `Q/n`, `K`, `M` and at least the two existing widths. Include a public longer real series, and explicitly synthetic controlled cases for mechanism isolation. Compare released, geometry, stream and the matched compiled dense control; retain an unchanged external comparator on a declared feasible subset. Report preparation and width-work diagnostics, absolute latency, numerical parity, and losses/crossover regions. Success means explaining observed behavior with measured work and exposing failures, not requiring stream to win every cell. A smaller but complete, prospectively fixed grid is preferable to selecting successful settings after measurement. This does not require a GPU, a DBMS integration, or new detector-accuracy claims.

2. **The dependence sensitivity is useful but still weaker than an independent-domain validation.** Sixty-two named recordings have appeared in earlier studies; the 36 SMD cases have nine label sequences and half share development geometry. Equal-label weighting and removal of development-matching geometry preserve the sign, so a simple duplicate-geometry objection is substantially answered. They do not identify all shared physical recordings or justify treating 73 series as 73 independent domains. For an evaluator, score rank/tie and label geometry matter more directly than whether the detector is fashionable; nevertheless, the paper has limited evidence outside those existing regimes. Sources: body lines 351–360, 444–450; primary v3 `shared_development_label_geometries` and `geometry_disjoint_from_development_sensitivity`.

   **Remediation:** preserve these conditional estimates and add a source-identified, geometry-distinct workload selected before new measurements, preferably serving the longer-series check above. Explicitly state its recording relationships. If a broader panel changes the sharing conclusion, narrow the deployment recommendation instead of averaging the contrary result away. Do not count copied channels, repeated scores or synthetic cases as extra independent datasets.

3. **Fresh reproduction remains an open empirical condition, and table generation needs a clearer contract.** [Run 40049786600](https://github.com/mandu5/sparse-vus/actions/runs/40049786600) was `in_progress` at inspection, with the fresh-isolated-reproduction step running and artifact upload pending. I do not count it as complete. The one-command driver does regenerate scores, execute both principal studies and reconstruct numerical summary JSON. However, it does not invoke the TeX table renderer or the resource study: `reproduce_pvldb20261024.py:23–24,94–111` lists the executed scripts, while `render_pvldb20261024_tables.py` separately reads the original v3 report filenames and resource report. The manuscript's phrase “reconstructs their tables” is therefore stronger than a literal rendered-table guarantee from that command.

   **Remediation:** retain a successful fresh completion receipt and downloadable audit outputs, report host-specific results without pooling hosts, and either wire a parameterized renderer to the replica outputs or state explicitly that the command produces the numerical tables as JSON. Give the resource reproduction command separately. A green workflow must be accompanied by the recorded complete cohort, correct source/array bindings, scalar/winner agreement and newly measured results; CI success by itself does not establish those scientific claims.

4. **Some significance language still needs precision.** A 0.01 VUS-PR regret cutoff is prospectively fixed, but the study does not validate it as a practical utility loss for applications. Likewise, the artifact's optional prefix bound is not empirically explained by measured `J_w` in this manuscript. Neither issue invalidates the main result. Sources: body lines 301–312 and 400–408.

   **Remediation:** describe 0.01 as the predeclared metric-scale cutoff unless application evidence is added. Either show visited-prefix diagnostics when discussing saturation losses or keep the explanation explicitly a hypothesis. The narrow exact-evaluator contribution does not require claims about downstream detection utility.

### Updated scientific judgment and remaining weaknesses

The initial three substantive conditions are now satisfied for the bounded manuscript: measured operating regimes including losses; longer, source-identified real workloads; and completed fresh primary/supplement reproduction with an accurate numerical-JSON contract. The new real panel is distinct from the 73-case primary cohort and spans substantially different lengths and component counts. It is **not** nine unseen populations: eight names were used before, and MITDB has seven label sequences. This answers the geometry/range concern without erasing dependence. No new mathematical defect was identified. The proof and the original independent small-case oracle check remain the basis for correctness; additional timing records do not prove the theorem.

The paper's strongest contribution is now adequately supported: a precise exact operator representation, an implementation whose benefit survives a matched compiled dense comparison, and a measured reduction of a substantial stored-profile workload. The primary and long-recording studies support the whole evaluator more convincingly than they support a major new batching advance.
