\title{Exact Evaluation of Time-Series Anomaly Metrics by Local Event Statistics}
\author{Youngmin Ko}
\email{rhdudals0505@naver.com}
\affiliation{\institution{Independent Researcher}\country{Republic of Korea}}
\begin{abstract}
Time-series anomaly evaluation repeatedly scans score thresholds to compute range-aware metrics. We derive local event statistics for two existing operators without changing their definitions. For dynamic VUS, inactive PR events vanish while ROC requires a predecessor correction; complete global ties and interval geometry give a support-sensitive exact representation. For flat reciprocal-cardinality range precision/recall, endpoint activations update both overlap and fragmentation statistics in constant time per inserted point. Exhaustive observed-threshold evaluation takes $O(n\log n+n)$, while $H$ ordered queries admit $O(n\log(H+1)+n+H)$ through ordinary offline bucketing. A frozen study on 73 real series and eight score procedures, including both operators, is reproduced on two CPU architectures. A separate range-metric study on the same 584 profiles compares a fused compiled raster with numerically matching outputs: exhaustive range-F1 evaluation improves geometrically by $27.8\times$, $21.4\times$ and $128.1\times$ on NAB, SMAP and SMD; fixed 100-query evaluation improves $1.72$--$1.99\times$. The 100-query approximation selects nonoverlapping winning models in 15/73 cases, twelve with range-F1 regret at least .01. Whole-consumer significance remains bounded: accelerating VUS alone in a public nine-metric workload gives only $1.031\times$ overall. We retain conventional-control wins, low-cardinality losses, source dependence and numerical limits. Classical component trees, sorting and caching are prior techniques; the contribution is the operator-specific exact state representation and its measured evaluation consequences.
\end{abstract}
